01 — Abstract

On Cognition, Culture, and Craft

Abstract

My practice starts from a simple conviction: nothing exists in isolation. Systems, people, ideas — everything is in constant conversation. The lenses you bring determine which conversations you can hear, and which you can start. I design at the intersection of cognitive & linguistic sciences, media arts & sciences, and education — three disciplines that share a single question: what creates meaning, and what does meaning create?

Years ago, while reading Guy Deutscher’s Through the Language Glass,[1] I discovered the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis. It’s a theory of cognitive science which argues that the language(s) you speak shape how you perceive the world; not a limit, but a lens. Simply put, learning new languages, new frameworks, new disciplines genuinely grows what you can see.

My background reflects this — spanning cognitive science, media arts, education, and hands-on making, each one shapes how I approach the others.

Culture and design work the same way. Culture shapes the meanings available to us; the things we make reshape the people who use them, which shapes what they make next. An unbroken cycle. In my work, I'm not just asking "what does the user want?" but "what does it shape?" This portfolio is a record of that exploration.

[1] Deutscher, G. (2010). Through the Language Glass: Why the World Looks Different in Other Languages. Metropolitan Books.

Interconnectivity

The condition — the state of being in relationship.

Revealing how meaning emerges from relationships between ideas, people, & technologies rather than from isolated elements. Culture shapes meaning; meaning shapes culture. Design lives in that loop.

Interaction

05 — About

About

Hi, I'm Lara. A designer who grew up between languages and cultures, learning early on that there's no neutral vantage point — only the one you happen to be standing at. Every framework you uncover doesn't replace the last one. It adds to it.

My studies followed the same logic: cognitive science, media arts, education, with detours wherever the question led. Less of a plan, more of a pattern. Find the interesting question and follow it into whatever field is holding the answer. Cognitive science taught me how people build meaning; media arts taught me how to build things, including prototypes in code. Both taught me that the best solution on paper isn't always the best one for the person using it.

Outside the screen: I cook from whatever's already there. The recipe is a starting point — suggestions, not requirements. That dish on the right is exactly that. The constraint, as always, turned out to be the interesting part.
